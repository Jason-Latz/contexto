Choosing a password you can actually remember

Every account you open asks you to invent a password, and every guide tells you the same thing: make it long, make it strange, and never write it on a note under the keyboard. The advice is sound, but people forget that a password is only one part of the story. The other part is the secret you keep about where and how you store it.

My grandmother kept her secret in a recipe book, between the soup and the bread. Nobody who opened that book was looking for anything but dinner, and that was the whole point. She wrote the code for the safe on the very last page, and she typed the code into the lock without ever looking down. She understood something that a browser with a saved tab and an open menu does not: a good hiding place looks boring.

So pick a password with care, guard the secret of where it lives, close the browser tab when you are done, and check the menu of your settings once a season. An icon on the screen is not a promise, and a symbol of safety is not the same as safety itself. Write your password down if you must, but treat the paper like money, because that is what it unlocks in the end.
